The Sun

The Sun is a huge star at the center of our solar system. It provides the light and heat that make life on Earth possible. The Sun is made mostly of hydrogen and helium and produces energy through nuclear fusion in its core.

Type G-type Star
Age ~4.6 Billion Years
Diameter ~1.39 Million km
Surface Temp. ~5,500°C
Core Temp. ~15 Million°C
Composition Hydrogen + Helium

🔥 Nuclear Fusion

Deep inside the Sun, hydrogen nuclei combine to form helium. This process releases enormous amounts of energy.

🌡️ Extremely Hot

The visible surface of the Sun is thousands of degrees Celsius, while its core reaches millions of degrees.

🌞 Solar Wind

The Sun constantly releases charged particles into space. This stream is called the solar wind.

🌍 Earth's Energy

Nearly all of Earth's natural energy ultimately comes from the Sun.

Enter one of the universe's most mysterious environments.

What Is a Black Hole?

A black hole is a region of space where gravity is extremely strong. Once something passes beyond its event horizon, it cannot escape back out.

Black holes can form when massive stars collapse at the end of their lives. Supermassive black holes can also exist at the centers of galaxies.

⚫

Stellar Black Holes

These can form from the collapse of massive stars and are usually several times the mass of our Sun.

🌌

Supermassive Black Holes

These enormous black holes can contain millions or billions of solar masses and are found at the centers of many galaxies.

🔄

Accretion Disk

Matter falling toward a black hole can form a rapidly rotating disk that becomes extremely hot and bright.

🚪

Event Horizon

The event horizon is the boundary around a black hole beyond which escape is impossible.

🧠 Black Hole Facts

01 — Black holes do not simply act like cosmic vacuum cleaners.
02 — Their gravity depends on their mass and distance.
03 — Light cannot escape from inside the event horizon.
04 — Black holes can merge with other black holes.
05 — Merging black holes can produce gravitational waves.
06 — Some black holes are surrounded by extremely hot matter.
07 — Supermassive black holes can sit at galaxy centers.
08 — Scientists can study black holes through their effects on nearby matter.

Stars Are Cosmic Powerhouses

Stars are enormous balls of extremely hot plasma held together by gravity. Most stars produce energy through nuclear fusion. They come in many different sizes, temperatures and colors.

The Sun is one example of a star, but the universe contains stars that are much smaller, much larger, hotter and cooler.

🔵

Blue Stars

Very hot and often massive stars. Their surfaces can reach tens of thousands of degrees.
